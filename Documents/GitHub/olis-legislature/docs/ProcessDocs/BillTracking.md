# Oregon Legislative Bill Process and Critical Deadlines

## Overview
This document outlines the specific process a bill goes through in the Oregon Legislature, with emphasis on critical deadlines that determine whether a bill remains alive or dies.

## Bill Process Flow with Critical Deadlines

### 1. Introduction Phase

#### Requirements
- **Sponsor Required**: Every bill must have a sponsor (member, committee, or by request)
- **Format**: Must be drafted by Legislative Counsel with proper formatting and impartial summary

#### Deadlines
**Long Session (2025)**:
- Drafting requests due: January 17, 2025 (5:00 PM)
- Introduction deadline: February 25, 2025 (5:00 PM)

**Short Session**:
- Governed by concurrent resolution adopted by previous Legislative Assembly

**House Specific (2021 session example)**:
- No introduction after 5:00 PM on 36th calendar day

#### Exceptions to Deadlines
- Committee-sponsored bills
- Ways & Means bills
- Member priority requests (5 per member in some sessions)
- Bills approved by presiding officer

### 2. First Reading and Committee Referral

#### Process
- Bill read by title only
- Immediately referred to committee by presiding officer

#### Critical Deadlines
- **Senate**: Must be referred within **7 calendar days** after First Reading
- **House**: Must be referred within **5-7 calendar days** (varies by session type)

#### Status Check
- If not referred within deadline → **BILL STATUS UNCERTAIN** (rules don't explicitly state consequence)

### 3. Committee Phase

#### Committee Actions Available
1. Hold public hearing (with notice requirements)
2. Hold work session to consider amendments
3. Report bill out with recommendation:
   - Do pass
   - Do pass with amendments
   - Do not pass
   - Without recommendation
4. Table the bill (effectively killing it)

#### Critical Deadlines

**Committee Work Deadlines**:
- Set by concurrent resolution for each session
- Committees must complete work by specified dates or bill dies

**Committee Report Filing Deadlines**:

**Senate Long Session**:
- Without amendments: 3 session days after committee action
- With amendments: 5 session days after committee action

**Senate Short Session**: 
- All bills: 3 session days after committee action

**House**:
- 2021 session: 3 session days after committee action
- 2022 session: 1 business day (no amendments) or 2 business days (with amendments)

### 4. Second Reading

#### Timing
- Bills without amendments: Next available calendar after committee report
- Bills with amendments: After amendments are distributed to members

#### Status
- No specific deadline that kills bills at this stage
- Primarily a procedural step before final consideration

### 5. Third Reading and Final Passage

#### Timing
- **Senate**: Next session day after Second Reading
- **House**: Session day following Second Reading

#### Requirements
- Roll call vote required
- Constitutional majority needed:
  - Senate: 16 votes
  - House: 31 votes
- Appropriation bills take precedence over other bills

### 6. Transmission to Other Chamber

#### Process
- Passed bills sent to other chamber
- Other chamber may:
  - Pass identical version
  - Pass with amendments
  - Defeat the bill

#### If Amended by Second Chamber
Original chamber must either:
1. Concur with amendments (requires constitutional majority)
2. Refuse to concur and request conference committee

## Critical "Kill Points" Where Bills Die

### 1. Failure to Meet Introduction Deadline
- Bills not introduced by deadline cannot proceed
- No mechanism to waive except specific exceptions

### 2. Committee Inaction
- Committee doesn't act by concurrent resolution deadline
- Committee tables the bill
- Committee reports "do not pass" and chamber adopts report

### 3. Indefinite Postponement
- Motion to indefinitely postpone passes
- Bill is dead for entire session
- Cannot be reconsidered

### 4. Failure to Pass Chamber
- Fails to get constitutional majority
- Can only be reconsidered if:
  - Member on prevailing side gives notice same day
  - Motion made next session day (or same day in short session)

### 5. End of Session (Sine Die)
- Any incomplete bill dies
- Bills do not carry over between sessions
- Must be reintroduced in new session

## Special Procedural Options

### Withdrawing from Committee
- Bypasses committee process
- Requirements:
  - **Senate**: 16 votes (constitutional majority)
  - **House**: 31 votes
- Bill proceeds directly to Second Reading

### Emergency Provisions (When Sine Die Imminent)
- Committee meeting notice: 1 hour minimum
- Amendment availability: 1 hour online minimum
- Expedited report filing

### Reconsideration Rules
- **Notice**: Must be given same day as vote
- **Motion timing**:
  - Long Session: Next session day
  - Short Session: Same day
- **Limit**: Only one reconsideration per vote
- **Who can move**: Only member who voted on prevailing side

## Bill Status Logic Framework

### ALIVE Status Indicators
- ✓ Within all applicable deadlines
- ✓ In committee with time remaining
- ✓ On calendar for reading
- ✓ Passed one chamber, proceeding to other
- ✓ In conference committee

### DEAD Status Indicators
- ✗ Past introduction deadline (unless excepted)
- ✗ Past committee deadline without action
- ✗ Tabled in committee
- ✗ "Do not pass" report adopted
- ✗ Indefinitely postponed
- ✗ Failed to pass (without valid reconsideration)
- ✗ Session ended (sine die)

### AT RISK Status Indicators
- ⚠ Approaching committee deadline
- ⚠ In committee late in session
- ⚠ Failed once but reconsideration notice given
- ⚠ In conference committee near sine die

## Implementation Notes for Status Tracking

1. **Track Current Date vs. Deadlines**
   - Introduction deadlines
   - Committee work deadlines (from concurrent resolution)
   - Days remaining in session

2. **Monitor Bill Location**
   - Which chamber
   - Which committee
   - On which calendar

3. **Check Recent Actions**
   - Committee reports filed
   - Votes taken
   - Motions made

4. **Calculate Time Remaining**
   - Session days (not calendar days) for most deadlines
   - Business days for some House deadlines
   - Hours for emergency provisions

This framework provides the logic needed to determine whether a bill is alive, dead, or at risk at any point in the legislative process.
